Answer

Is solar plus battery worth it for Karimunjawa? The island runs on diesel.

Yes. Solar plus battery cuts the cost of electricity on Karimunjawa from 0.511 to 0.277 USD/kWh (-45.7%).

  • Near tie: 8 designs are within 1.0% of the least cost, from 7,500 to 9,000 kWp of PV.
  • Reserve case: if 1 diesel unit(s) must run in every hour, the least cost design is 4,250 kWp with 11,000 kWh at 0.461 USD/kWh, and the main design would cost 0.533 USD/kWh.
  • Diesel unit size: with units of 2,700 kW instead, diesel only costs 0.594 USD/kWh and the least cost design is 9,000 kWp with 19,000 kWh at 0.287 USD/kWh (51.7% below diesel only); if 1 diesel unit(s) must run in every hour, that design would cost 0.690 USD/kWh and the least cost design is 2,750 kWp with 8,000 kWh at 0.565 USD/kWh.
  • Island prices: PV and battery capital prices can rise 364.6% (PV to 5,199 USD/kWp, battery to 1,812 USD/kWh) before diesel only is cheaper.
  • Diesel engine hours: the least cost design cuts engine hours by 87.7% (1,080 against 8,760 unit hours a year), so overhaul and engine replacement costs, left out here, would favour it.
  • Land: the main design needs 9.8 ha of the 9.0 ha set aside (fits: no).
  • Published design size: 1,244 kWp of PV with no battery gives 0.477 USD/kWh.
  • Cost: this study used 0.10380 USD of model API calls, of which 0.10367 USD was the web search that sourced its inputs.

Second model review

muse-wonder-1.0

  • Headline least-cost design of 8,500 kWp PV is not buildable on the stated land: it needs 9.8 ha versus 9.0 ha set aside, exceeding the 7,830 kWp that fits; the land-constrained alternative is 7,750 kWp at 0.279 USD/kWh. (How firm is the answer)
  • The 0.277 USD/kWh headline assumes diesel is off for 7,680 h/yr; with a reserve diesel unit always on, the same design costs 0.533 USD/kWh and the reserve-case optimum falls to 49.3% renewable share, so the saving depends on unproven no-diesel grid operation. (How firm is the answer)
  • A basic plant fact is unresolved: diesel units are modelled as 1800.0 kW although sources also print 2,700 kW; the alternative changes diesel-only LCOE from 0.511 to 0.594 USD/kWh and pushes the least-cost PV size to 9,000 kWp on the search-grid edge. (How firm is the answer)
  • Weather uncertainty materially changes the optimum: GHI ranges from 1,731 to 2,049 kWh/m2/yr (18.4% spread), with least-cost PV from 7,250 to 8,500 kWp and LCOE from 0.233 to 0.277 USD/kWh depending on source. (Range of the answer)
  • Fuel price 1.26 USD/L is explicitly a pre-tax, pre-sea-transport depot floor, with source values ranging 1.03 to 1.64 USD/L; delivered island fuel cost therefore is not established for the 0.511 USD/kWh diesel baseline or the 0.24 USD/L breakeven claim. (Inputs)
  • Diesel economics rest on assumed, unvalidated parameters — fuel slope 0.25 L/kWh, intercept 0.08 L/h per kW rated, and diesel O&M 0.03 USD/kWh with capital treated as sunk — and no sensitivity tests these assumptions despite diesel-only fuel of 3,653,468 L/yr driving the comparison. (open)
  • Up-front capital of 18,489,000 USD uses generic prices such as PV capex 1119.0 USD/kWp and battery capex 390.0 USD/kWh, while island shipping, local labour and construction are excluded; remote-island delivered and installed cost is therefore unpriced. (Not covered by this study)
  • Battery duty is severe at 263 cycles/yr over a 20-year project with a 15-year battery life, yet degradation beyond a replacement rule and salt-air corrosion are excluded from the 0.277 USD/kWh result. (Not covered by this study)
  • Demand basis needs reconciliation: 9,568,110 kWh/yr with peak 1,422 kW implies a 76.8% load factor, while the underlying Hiron 2021 checks flag an impossible PV yield of 4,291 kWh/kWp, battery output exceeding input, and inconsistent totals; no section explains which Hiron values remain trustworthy for load and plant sizing. (open)
  • Reported reliability is only hourly energy balance with 0 kWh/yr unserved against an allowed unserved fraction of 0.001; the study does not model forced outages, N-1 loss of the largest diesel or battery inverter, diesel start costs or minimum up/down times, or sub-hourly ramps despite 163 starts/yr in the least-cost design. (open)

Not covered by this study

  • Grid stability: frequency and voltage control, grid forming inverters and protection for hours with no diesel running
  • Land ownership, permits and the environmental review
  • Site layout, shading, soil and access for construction
  • Shipping to the island, local labour and construction time
  • Financing terms, tariffs, subsidies and who pays
  • Demand growth beyond the growth case, and new loads such as electric boats
  • Battery ageing beyond the replacement rule, and salt air corrosion
  • Diesel price risk beyond the sensitivity table
  • Diesel overhaul and engine replacement costs, which grow with engine hours
